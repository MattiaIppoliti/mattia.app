POC AI isybank

Paying an F24 tax form in a banking app means filling a grid of codes. The tax code for the property tax, the section, the municipality code, the instalment code. Almost nobody knows them by heart, and one wrong digit sends the money to the wrong treasury.

Situation

isybank is the digital bank of Intesa Sanpaolo. Its customers move money, hunt for documents and pay taxes through menus, and the tax forms are the worst of it. The F24 asks for a tax code, a section, a municipality code and an instalment code before it accepts a single euro.

Task

Find out whether a generative assistant could carry out real operations from one spoken sentence rather than answer questions about them. The proof of concept had to live inside the app, work by voice or keyboard, and end with money actually moving.

Action

I built a conversational layer over the existing banking services. It reads the intent, pulls the fields out of the sentence, and closes the gaps on its own. It finds an IBAN in the address book, turns the word IMU into tax codes 3912 through 3915, resolves Biella into municipality code A859. Then it stops, shows what it is about to do, and waits. Nothing executes without a confirmation, and the assistant asks for one field at a time when a form needs more than the sentence carried.

Result

One demo run covers four flows: a scheduled transfer, a search through unread documents, a filter over outgoing transactions, and an F24 payment. 50 â‚¬ to a named payee with the reason "cena", dated to the next day, executed after a single confirmation. The F24 came back precompiled with twelve fields, and the bank's own system posted the 200 â‚¬ payment and sent the usual notification. The customer typed no field values at any point.

The language model is the least interesting part of this. What made it usable is the pause before execution: seven questions on the F24, each answered with a yes, then a full summary of the twelve fields on screen. A wrong tax code is not a wrong answer, it is money in the wrong place, so the assistant would rather ask again than guess.
