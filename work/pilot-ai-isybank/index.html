Pilot AI Intelligence isybank

Say the sentence, confirm the summary, the payment goes out.

Role

Year

isybank is the digital bank of Intesa Sanpaolo. Everything its customers do runs through menus, and the tax forms are the worst of it. An F24 wants a tax code, a section, a municipality code and an instalment code before it accepts a single euro. Almost nobody knows those by heart, and one wrong digit sends the money to the wrong treasury.

The brief was one assistant that answers whatever a customer asks about their own money, by voice or by typing, and then acts on the answer. Ask it to cross two things and it draws the comparison: how much went on healthy food against junk food last month, plotted rather than described. Ask narrower and it answers narrower, the last few transactions in one category, or how the investments are doing. Ask it to find a document and it searches the archive and returns the statement the request points to.

From there it acts. Transfers and F24 forms are carried out through the chat in steps. It can also read a profile, either described by the customer or inferred from how they actually spend, recommend the card that suits it, and take the purchase of that card plan to the end without leaving the conversation. Revolut shipped this shape of product as AIR about two years later, one chat that handles cards, subscriptions and portfolio questions. We were building it inside a retail bank in 2023, which is a harder room to do it in.

My team and I built a conversational layer over the existing banking services. Six of us: two on the front end, three on the back end, and me. It reads the intent, pulls the fields out of the sentence, and closes the gaps on its own. It finds an IBAN in the address book, turns the word IMU into tax codes 3912 through 3915, resolves Biella into municipality code A859, categorises transactions so a spending question has something to compare. When the sentence carries less than a form needs, it asks for one field at a time instead of throwing a blank form back at you. Then it stops, shows what it is about to do, and waits. Nothing executes without a confirmation.

One demo run walks through four of these end to end: a scheduled transfer, a search through unread documents, a filter over outgoing transactions, and an F24 payment. Fifty euro to a named payee with the reason "dinner", dated to the next day, sent after a single confirmation. The F24 came back precompiled across twelve fields, and the bank's own system posted the two hundred euro payment and pushed the usual notification. The customer typed no field values at any point.

The language model is the least interesting part of this. What makes it usable is the pause before execution: seven questions on the F24, each answered with a yes, then the full summary of twelve fields on screen. A wrong tax code is not a wrong answer, it is money in the wrong place, so the assistant would rather ask again than guess.
